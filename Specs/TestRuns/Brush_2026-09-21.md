# Brush benchmark, 2026-09-21

`RedPrism.exe --bench brush` (Release, x64) on the C10 closeout build, the legacy scenario from
archived brush benchmark notes: a 4000 × 4000 document, 40 px and 800 px soft (0 % hardness) white strokes of
120 pointer updates 40 document pixels apart, on a blank layer and on an opaque 4K layer, at fit zoom in the
default 1280 × 800 window (hidden). Machine: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5080 (driver 32.0.16.1664) on a 120 Hz display,
AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D, Windows 11 26200; WARP is the Microsoft Basic Render Driver on the same CPU.

Columns: `update` is one pointer update (curve, compute dispatch, preview readback, tile composition and tile
upload) when every update is followed by a presented frame; `frame` adds that frame (composition of the 4K
document, blit, Present); `alone` is the update without a frame in between; `commit` is mouse-up (settle, snapshot,
history, adoption) and `mouseUp` adds the frame that shows it. Median / p95 / max over 240 updates (two passes),
commit over the two passes of that kind. Presenting paces the GPU queue at the display's refresh and the preview
readback waits behind the previous frame, so `update` and `frame` sit at the refresh interval (8.3 ms at 120 Hz)
whatever the brush does; `alone` is the brush's own cost.

## Reading

- Every pass committed one `Brush Stroke` entry; mouse-up never assembled the layer (the self-test and
  `DocumentTests` `snapshot.*` assert `IsMaterialized()` stays false; the commit times above include the
  thumbnail and the GPU adoption of the working texture).
- Against the legacy Mac numbers (Debug, Metal: 40 px 0.36–0.47 ms median with 1.3–4.8 ms mouse-up; 800 px
  2.64–3.12 ms median, 3.70 ms p95, 8.1–9.7 ms mouse-up on a blank layer; Release 800 px 2.81 ms median, 9.6–11.0 ms
  mouse-up) the 40 px brush matches (0.43 ms median, 3.8 ms commit) and the 800 px brush is within the order of
  magnitude the plan asked for but about four times slower per update (11.9 ms median) and two to three times
  slower at mouse-up (22–27 ms). An 800 px tip stepping 40 px touches up to twenty 256² tiles per update; each
  costs a compute dispatch, a 256 KiB `uint` preview readback through a staging buffer, the CPU composition of the
  tile and a 256 KiB `UpdateSubresource`, followed by `GenerateMips` over the whole 4K working texture.
- Not a shipping gate: the plan's WARP p95 ≤ 8 ms was explicitly not one, and no Photoshop parity is claimed.
  Follow-ups worth measuring, in order of expected gain: a one-byte preview format (R8 typed UAV) to cut the
  readback and the CPU composition input by four; regenerating mips only for the touched region or deferring them
  until the frame; reading the preview back at the next frame instead of inside the pointer update so the update
  never waits for the previous Present; composing the tile on the GPU as the legacy kernel did.

## Reproduce

```powershell
.\build.ps1 -Platform x64 -Configuration Release
.\.build\x64\Release\RedPrism.exe --bench brush
.\.build\x64\Release\RedPrism.exe --warp --bench brush
```

`BRUSH BENCH` lines go to the redirected stdout (and the debugger); the process exits 0.
